Limbo: Reconnecting Post-Grad Friendships Through Asynchronous Prompts

How mixed-methods research turned a 0% retention drop-off into a group-based social protocol for post-grads.

Overview

When young adults graduate college and disperse across cities, they lose the forced proximity that naturally sustains relationships. Digital interactions regress into forced, high-effort “catch-up” calls or performative social broadcasts.

Limbo is a daily prompt-based social space for small, private circles. Instead of requiring users to initiate conversations from scratch, Limbo acts as a low-friction protocol—delivering structured, playful, and vulnerable prompts that spark effortless daily contact.

01 — Problem

The “Limbo Zone” of Post-Grad Friendships

Post-grad life strips away spontaneous interaction. Friendships enter an inactive “Limbo Zone”—where both parties want to reach out, but neither does due to high activation energy, lack of shared context, and fear of being overbearing.

Through initial research, three critical gaps in adult social life emerged:

01

Structure

A repeatable, low-effort reason to show up daily without overthinking.

02

Permission

A socially safe excuse to reach out without needing a major life update.

03

Randomness

Recreating the serendipity of late-night dorm debates and weird conversations.

02 — Strategy

Mixed-Methods Research Pipeline

To de-risk the product from concept to live build, I designed a multi-stage research pipeline matching methodology to product maturity:

01

Foundational Needfinding (n = 11)

In-depth qualitative interviews with recent college grads to understand communication friction.

02

Prompt Bias & Preference Testing (n = 18 & n = 23)

Quantitative bias sorting and wording sensitivity tests to determine ideal prompt tones, alongside a survey on private vs. public digital space norms.

03

Live Behavioral Alpha (n = 20)

Field diary study deploying a live iOS app via TestFlight to evaluate engagement loops and daily prompt retention rhythms.

03 — Insights & Pivots

Research-Driven Product Triads

Triad 1: Overcoming the “High-Stakes” Catch-Up Call

The Insight: New grads avoid initiating contact because calls feel forced and require macro life updates (jobs, moving).

“When you don’t see a person that often, you’re like, well, the time we have together, I guess we’re just gonna talk about catching up.” — Skye, Study Participant

My Action: Championed “Prompts as Protocol” to grant users low-stakes, daily “social permission” to reach out without needing a big life update.

Product Outcome: Implemented asynchronous Daily Prompts that expire every 24 hours, giving friends a fresh, low-effort reason to connect.

Triad 2: Prompt Wording & Tone Sensitivity

The Insight: Quantitative prompt sorting (n = 18) proved generic questions caused boredom, while overly personal questions induced anxiety. Top-ranked prompts sparked playful debate or imaginative scenario-building.

  1. Random Hobby: “What random hobby have you always thought about trying?” (4.30 Avg Rank)
  2. Alternate Scenario: “If you woke up in someone else’s body, how would you convince your loved ones?” (4.40 Avg Rank)
  3. Lowkey Scared: “What are you still lowkey scared of?” (5.30 Avg Rank)

My Action: Categorized prompts by depth and engineered rotating multi-modal prompt formats (text, photo proof, drawing canvas, and binary debate sliders).

Product Outcome: Developed dynamic “Prompt Decks” allowing users to cycle through low-friction input modes.

Triad 3: Diagnosing the Alpha Drop-Off & Pivoting Onboarding

The Insight: Live TestFlight telemetry (n = 20) showed 55% completed at least 1 prompt, but 0% sustained a 7-day streak. Qualitative follow-ups revealed an “Isolation Penalty”—users who joined without their existing friend group found the feed empty and stopped responding. Viewing friends’ responses (5.1/10 motivation) was a stronger driver than individual self-expression (4.3/10).

My Action: Re-analyzed diary feedback and persuaded the cross-functional team to abandon individual user onboarding in favor of social-graph cohort onboarding.

Product Outcome: Redesigned onboarding to require pre-formed micro-groups (3–15 friends) before app entry (onboarding 37 beta testers in cohorts) and introduced “Limbo Zone” profile status badges for dormant friends (14+ days).

04 — Final Impact

The Shipped Experience & Behavioral Outcomes

01

Multi-Modal Engagement

Reduced average prompt completion time from 60 seconds (text only) to under 10 seconds (slider debates & canvas draws).

02

Living Profile Artifacts

Every answered prompt builds a personal grid of micro-memories over time, creating an authentic digital footprint rather than a polished highlight reel.

03

Real-World Activation

Hosted a live “Limbo IRL” event with physical prompt cards and QR onboarding to seed group context prior to app usage.

Reflections

Key Takeaways for Social UXR

1 · Evaluate social graphs, not just solo users.

In social apps, individual usability testing is insufficient. Product performance depends on social graph density and network dynamics.

2 · Value intellectual honesty in data.

Spotlighting an early 0% retention drop-off and diagnosing its root cause built the trust necessary to drive a critical product pivot.

3 · Cross-functional operations.

Bringing developers into user research sessions made the pivot fast, seamless, and aligned with engineering timelines.
