Overview

When young adults graduate college and disperse across cities, they lose the forced proximity that naturally sustains relationships. Digital interactions regress into forced, high-effort “catch-up” calls or performative social broadcasts.

Limbo is a daily prompt-based social space for small, private circles. Instead of requiring users to initiate conversations from scratch, Limbo acts as a low-friction protocol—delivering structured, playful, and vulnerable prompts that spark effortless daily contact.

01 — Problem

The “Limbo Zone” of Post-Grad Friendships

Post-grad life strips away spontaneous interaction. Friendships enter an inactive “Limbo Zone”—where both parties want to reach out, but neither does due to high activation energy, lack of shared context, and fear of being overbearing.

Through initial research, three critical gaps in adult social life emerged:

01

Structure

A repeatable, low-effort reason to show up daily without overthinking.

02

Permission

A socially safe excuse to reach out without needing a major life update.

03

Randomness

Recreating the serendipity of late-night dorm debates and weird conversations.

02 — Strategy

Mixed-Methods Research Pipeline & Tactical Execution

To de-risk the product from concept to live build, I designed a multi-stage research pipeline matching methodology to product maturity:

01

Foundational Needfinding (n = 11)

Execution: Conducted semi-structured qualitative interviews with recent grads, using grounded theory coding to map emotional barriers around post-grad friendship decay.

02

Prompt Bias, Risk & Preference Testing (n = 18 & n = 23)

Research Engineering: Built a custom interactive bias-sorting web interface using Claude to dynamically test prompt categories, enabling n = 18 participants to perform ordinal rankings across risk vs. engagement tiers.

Quantitative Norm Survey: Distributed an n = 23 survey evaluating digital privacy norms (Close Friends vs. broadcast feeds).

03

Live Behavioral Alpha (n = 20)

Research Ops & Telemetry: Deployed a live React Native build to TestFlight, logging daily prompt completion rates and running exit diary interviews to diagnose retention drop-off.

03 — Insights & Pivots

Research-Driven Product Triads

Triad 1: Overcoming the “High-Stakes” Catch-Up Call

The Insight: New grads avoid initiating contact because calls feel forced and require macro life updates (jobs, moving).

“When you don’t see a person that often, you’re like, well, the time we have together, I guess we’re just gonna talk about catching up.” — Skye, Study Participant

My Action: Championed “Prompts as Protocol” to grant users low-stakes, daily “social permission” to reach out without needing a big life update.

Product Outcome: Implemented asynchronous Daily Prompts that expire every 24 hours, giving friends a fresh, low-effort reason to connect.

Triad 2: Prompt Wording & Ordinal Preference Ranking

The Insight: Quantitative prompt ranking (n = 18, measured on a 1-to-10 ordinal scale where 1 = Most Preferred) demonstrated that generic questions caused boredom, while overly vulnerable prompts induced anxiety without prior rapport.

Top-Ranked Prompts (Lower Average Rank = Higher Participant Preference):

  1. Random Hobby: “What random hobby have you always thought about trying?” (Avg Rank: 4.30)
  2. Alternate Scenario: “If you woke up in someone else’s body, how would you convince your loved ones?” (Avg Rank: 4.40)
  3. Lowkey Scared: “What are you still lowkey scared of?” (Avg Rank: 5.30)

My Action: Categorized prompts into distinct risk tiers (Cold Hooks vs. Deep Questions) and structured multi-modal input options (text, photo proof, drawing canvas, and binary debate sliders).

Product Outcome: Developed dynamic “Prompt Decks” cycling through distinct input modes based on group safety levels.

Triad 3: Diagnosing the Alpha Drop-Off & Pivoting Onboarding

The Insight: Live TestFlight telemetry (n = 20) showed 55% completed at least 1 prompt, but 0% sustained a 7-day streak. Qualitative follow-ups revealed an “Isolation Penalty”—users joining without an active friend network found empty feeds and stopped responding. Viewing friends’ responses (5.1/10 motivation) outscored individual self-expression (4.3/10 motivation) as the primary driver to return.

My Action: Re-analyzed diary feedback and persuaded the cross-functional team to abandon individual user onboarding in favor of social-graph cohort onboarding.

Product Outcome: Restructured onboarding to require pre-formed micro-groups (3–15 friends) before app entry (onboarding 37 beta testers across structured cohorts) and introduced “Limbo Zone” profile status badges for dormant ties.

04 — Product Scope

The Final Tested Product Experience

01

Multi-Modal Engagement

Prompts support flexible formats including drawing canvases, text inputs, photo proofs, and binary slider debates.

02

Living Profile Artifacts

Every answered prompt builds a personal grid of micro-memories over time, creating an authentic digital footprint rather than a polished highlight reel.

03

Real-World Activation

Hosted a live “Limbo IRL” event with physical prompt cards and QR onboarding to seed group context prior to app usage.

Reflections & Limitations

Key Takeaways

1 · Evaluate social graphs, not just solo users.

In social apps, individual usability testing is insufficient. Product utility depends on social graph density and network dynamics.

2 · Value intellectual honesty in data.

Spotlighting an early 0% retention drop-off and diagnosing its root cause built the trust necessary to drive a critical product pivot.

Study Limitations

Small Alpha Sample Size (n = 20)

While the field alpha provided rich qualitative diagnostics on retention drop-off, n = 20 across a short period is a small sample for longitudinal retention claims.

Unmeasured Post-Pivot Retention

Beta onboarding succeeded in placing 37 testers into structured group cohorts; however, long-term 30-day retention across these cohorts was not quantitatively measured within the 10-week capstone timeline.
