01Problem

How do recent graduates maintain friendships after losing the physical proximity granted by college?

Limbo is a daily-prompt social app for recent graduates. My goal was to understand how friendships hold up after college, and what makes a digital space feel intimate enough to share everyday minutia, to inform product strategy and behavior design over 20 weeks.

02Methodology

Semi-structured Interviews

Coded thematically

20+interviews
Question

How do friendships change after graduation?

What I found

Friendships stall in a “limbo zone” because no one has permission to reach out. People needed:

Structure
A built-in reason and rhythm for staying in touch.

Permission
Reaching out without a big update to justify it.

Randomness
Small, unplanned moments of contact.

03Key Insight

Intimacy in distanced relationships and social media comes from the trust implied by exposure to everyday minutia, because one is sharing more than “performance” and milestones with another.

Main account

“Main is like even your grandmother and professor can see.”

“A lot of people follow me that I don’t really know.”

Private space

“Main: performative. Close: authentic.”

“It means they deem you close enough to want you to know about the small things in their life.”

04Impact

This generative + evaluative research informed platform design & behavior guidelines, some of which included:

01

Design for intimacy in 1:1 contexts and play in group contexts

Comments are direct DMs, not public, to start conversation. Party mode and customizable games in the Circles feature.

02

Increase solo network benefit

Promote engagement without requiring users to add lots of people, which dilutes the intimacy of their network: Explore page, Lightning Round takes, Archive, Personal Scrapbook.

03

Require mutual buy-in to establish trust

The feed unlocks only after you answer the daily prompt.

04

Subvert expectations for excitement

Reactions, not likes, no public follower count, and questions that are interesting to answer and make users want to see others’ answers.
