Overview

When young adults graduate college and scatter across cities, they lose the forced proximity that naturally sustains relationships. Staying in touch often degrades into awkward, high-effort catch-up calls or silent social media scrolling.

Limbo was created as a low-friction protocol for post-grad friendships. Designed to support both 1-on-1 friendships and close-knit groups, Limbo delivers daily, low-stakes prompts (text, drawings, photo proofs, and opinion debates) that grant friends social permission to interact effortlessly without needing a major life update to reach out.

01 — Problem & Audit

The Post-Grad Friendship Gap & Competitive Audit

Through foundational interviews with recent grads (Stanford, SDSU), we mapped the core barriers to post-grad social connection: high activation energy, lack of shared daily context, and fear of being overbearing.

Competitive Analysis: Daily Check-In Mechanics

To understand how existing platforms attempt daily engagement, I conducted a competitive audit of platforms like BeReal and Candle:

The Flaw in Existing Models

Daily notification mechanics often devolve into broadcast performance or obligation.

The Private Space Opportunity

Survey synthesis showed that while public feeds (Instagram) feel performative ("milestones"), intimate connections thrive in private, lower-exposure spaces (Close Friends, Finstas) where users can share unpolished, everyday moments.

02 — Research Pipeline

Mixed-Methods Strategy & Execution

I designed an iterative research pipeline matching methodology to each stage of product maturity:

01

Needfinding Interviews (n = 11 Total Team; n = 2 Individual 90-Min Deep Dives)

Conducted extensive 90-minute qualitative interviews with recent Stanford and SDSU grads to map emotional friction, loss of proximity, and communication decay after college.

02

Private Spaces in Public Media Survey (n = 23)

Distributed across Reddit and community mailing lists to analyze how young adults construct digital intimacy and differentiate public broadcasts from private friend circles.

03

Interactive Preference Sorter & Interviews (n = 18)

Research Engineering: Built a custom interactive prompt sorter in Claude to run head-to-head preference rankings across 4 prompt categories (Hypotheticals, Opinions, Personal, and Debates), pairing each test with a 1-on-1 qualitative interview.

04

7-Day Beta Diary Waves

Deployed early App Store builds to test daily response behavior, track prompt skip rates, and gather daily diary feedback.

03 — Insights & Mechanics

Finding 1: Designing for Intimacy vs. Designing for Play

What I Found: Research revealed a clear divide in user expectations—one-on-one interactions required high intimacy, while group settings required playfulness and low pressure.

What We Built:

  • Private Comments as Direct Messages: Commenting on a friend's prompt answer opens a private 1-on-1 DM rather than a public thread, fostering direct conversation.
  • Playful Input Modes: Added a Drawing Canvas, binary debate sliders, and group game modes to keep group interactions light.

Finding 2: Prompt Taxonomy & Overcoming the "Icebreaker" Stigma

What I Found: In the preference ranking study, Personal and Opinion prompts ranked significantly higher than generic icebreakers. Participants noted that generic "conversation starters" felt clinical and awkward when used with existing friends.

What We Built: Curated a master prompt taxonomy focusing on creative, surprising angles (e.g., "What's your most unfair ick?" or "If you woke up in someone else's body, how would you convince your loved ones?") rather than basic interview questions.

Finding 3: Solving the Cold-Start Problem with Lock Mechanics & Profile Scrapbooks

What I Found: Beta testing showed that when users logged in and saw an empty feed, engagement stalled. Seeing friends' answers was the primary motivation to open the app.

What We Built:

  • Lock Mechanism: Users must post their own daily response to unlock and view their friends' answers for that day.
  • Profile Scrapbooks: Created a personal archive grid of past prompt answers and pinned memories, giving users content to explore even when friends were inactive.

04 — Go-To-Market & Launch

Research-Backed Campus Takeover & Generative Brand Strategy

To transition Limbo from a digital prototype into a real-world social movement, I translated research principles directly into our Go-To-Market (GTM) strategy:

AI Generative Brand Exploration

Used AI image generation tools to prototype a bold, raw, and high-energy visual identity—building mood collages for subway ads, apparel patches, and pop-up experience concepts.

Targeted Campus Poster Takeover

Selected 24 top-performing research prompts and printed them on bright, high-contrast paper. Posted them overnight across high-friction transition locations—graduate residences, all-gender restrooms, library stacks, and student unions.

Interactive QR Entry

Each poster featured a QR code allowing students to answer the prompt instantly and view live campus responses without download friction.

Reflection & Limitations

Key Takeaways

0-to-1 Systems Thinking

Building a successful social product requires evaluating the full ecosystem—combining behavioral research, UI mechanics, brand atmosphere, and real-world onboarding tactics.

Value of Proactive Research Engineering

Building custom tools (like the Claude prompt sorter) allows researchers to gather quantitative preference data quickly without waiting for full engineering builds.

Study Limitations

Unmeasured Long-Term Retention

While cohort onboarding succeeded during launch, tracking 30-day longitudinal retention was constrained by the 22-week capstone timeline.

Early Needfinding Constraints

Initial deep-dive qualitative interviews relied on a small sample (n = 2 led individually out of n = 11 team total), requiring heavy validation through broader survey distribution (n = 23) and beta feedback.
