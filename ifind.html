Overview

The Filming Interactions to Nurture Development (FIND) program is an early childhood intervention program that records parent-child interactions to coach caregivers on key developmental techniques (such as "serve and return" and "naming").

To scale this research, the lab developed iFind—an automated software tool designed to take 60-minute raw video sessions, use AI to identify behavioral timestamps, and trim clips into structured coaching videos.

As a UX Research Assistant, I ran usability studies to evaluate how participants navigated the beta tool, identified friction points in the AI-assisted editing flow, and optimized our team's research ops.

01 — Context & Problem

The Challenge: Translating Domain Expertise to AI Workflows

Behavioral video editing requires strict adherence to lab coding definitions. Early usability testing revealed two primary challenges:

Information & Domain Gaps

Participants struggled to grasp specific behavioral definitions (like "serve and return") during tasks, leading to editing errors unrelated to the UI itself.

The AI Trust Gap

Users were hesitant to trust AI clip suggestions. Rather than wanting an automated tool that replaced their decisions, users wanted full agency to review, override, and manually fine-tune edits.

02 — Research Ops

Usability Testing Setup & Methodology

Sample & Recruitment

Screened participants across university mailing lists, tracking prior video editing experience to observe behavioral differences between novice and experienced editors.

Testing Protocol

Conducted 50 moderated sessions using a structured task script and the think-aloud method to capture live mental models, feature discovery order, and information architecture clarity.

Onboarding & Script Auditing

Audited testing scripts to enforce precise lab terminology (standardizing "returns" instead of "responds") and created visual concept diagrams for "naming" mechanics.

03 — Key Insights & Changes

Insight 1: Users Lacked Confidence in AI Automation Without Control

The Finding: Participants hesitated to rely on AI clip suggestions when they couldn't preview edits against the raw video.

The Change: Partnered with engineering to introduce dual-view tabs, allowing users to seamlessly toggle between raw 60-minute footage and trimmed clip previews.

Insight 2: Conceptual Confusion Hindered Task Completion

The Finding: The first 6 participants struggled to differentiate "serve and return" behaviors during tasks despite verbal introductions.

The Change: Created visual reference graphics and an in-app "cheat sheet" outlining goal definitions, clip structures, and behavioral examples. Subsequent participants completed tasks without conceptual confusion.

Insight 3: Disrupted Mental Models in Video Editing

The Finding: Experienced editors expected standard UI patterns (e.g., clicking timeline text bubbles to edit text, receiving download status indicators, seeing default timeline templates).

The Change: Identified heuristic violations (such as missing download visibility status) and updated the UI layout to include pre-structured timeline templates and top-anchored notifications.

04 — Engineering Velocity

Unblocking the Design-to-Engineering Feedback Loop

During initial testing, software updates lagged because engineers had to manually translate long document notes into visual UI changes. As a result, 12 participants would test the exact same build before a fix was deployed.

My Action

Leveraged my design background to build live, interactive Figma prototypes that mapped exact interaction changes, animation styles, and layout shifts alongside our qualitative notes.

The Outcome

Eliminated interpretation guesswork for developers, dramatically speeding up software updates and reducing the feedback cycle from 12 users per iteration down to 4.

Reflection & Limitations

What I Learned

Research Ops is Product Strategy

Standardizing repository structures, scheduling calendars, and note-taking formats is essential for maintaining research quality across multiple facilitators.

Bridge the Dev Gap with Visuals

Delivering raw qualitative text to engineers creates a visualization bottleneck. Providing interactive prototypes alongside insights accelerates implementation.

Project Limitations & Future Opportunities

Unmeasured Quantitative Metrics

Due to early project scope, task completion rates and time-on-task were not systematically tracked quantitatively. Future iterations should incorporate task completion percentages and System Usability Scale (SUS) benchmarks.

Small Build Iteration Windows

Early engineering constraints limited the speed at which major backend AI improvements could be shipped during live testing rounds.
