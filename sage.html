Designing context-aware AI that meets impulse spending in the moment it happens.

Executive Summary

Existing personal finance platforms focus heavily on numerical budgeting and post-hoc expense tracking — but impulse spending is overwhelmingly driven by real-time emotional states, cognitive fatigue, and environmental context.

Sage is a conversational AI financial coach designed to curb impulse spending by fostering mindful awareness, real-time reflection, and proactive intention-setting. As Lead UX Researcher on a 4-person team, I drove the qualitative and quantitative research roadmap — from secondary literature synthesis and baseline study design through behavioral personas, assumption testing, interaction wireframing, brand strategy, and evaluative usability testing.

Phase 1 — Problem Definition & Secondary Research

Grounding the strategy in behavioral science.

Literature review & behavioral frameworks

To ground the team’s product strategy in proven behavioral science, I analyzed core behavioral literature and synthesized two key frameworks:

01

Emotional & contextual cues

Evaluated research (Wen et al., Rodrigues et al.) showing how environmental triggers (sales notifications) and psychological states (FOMO, stress, fatigue) bypass rational spending limits.

02

Intervention mechanisms

Mapped research (Moser et al., Peetz & Davydenko) on proactive strategies, micro-friction, and personalized reflection to address the root drivers of impulse behavior.

Comparative market positioning

To identify white space in the market, I curated an extensive list of budgeting, cashback, and manual finance-management tools, then mapped them across a 2×2 matrix:

01

Timing of intervention

Reactive (after spending) vs. preventative (before / during spending).

02

User effort required

Passive / automated vs. active / manual.

Market Opportunity

Existing tools either act reactively after money is spent or demand high manual effort that leads to user fatigue. Together, the team positioned Sage in the preventative + low-friction guided space — intervening before the point of purchase.

Phase 2 — Baseline Research & Behavioral Insights

Capturing how impulse spending actually happens.

Baseline study & quantitative metrics

I designed and executed a multi-stage baseline study to capture real-world spending habits:

01

Screening protocol

Built a screening protocol (N = 39) to recruit active convenience spenders making 2–3+ daily discretionary purchases.

02

Diary study

Authored the daily diary protocol (N = 7, 5-day logging), pre/post interview scripts, and logging prompts.

03

Data synthesis

Led affinity-mapping sessions across dozens of logged impulse incidents — food, delivery, online retail, and impulsive subscription sign-ups.

Persona: the Guilt-Driven Impulse Buyer

Synthesizing baseline data, I created the primary behavioral persona to align the team’s design decisions.

01

Core profile

Young professionals / students (ages 20–30) experiencing high emotional volatility with money.

02

The behavioral loop

Spontaneous purchases feel justified in the moment but lead to severe post-purchase guilt. Traditional budget apps are actively avoided because they trigger shame.

User journey mapping

I mapped the persona’s 5-stage behavioral journey to isolate intervention points:

  1. Buildup — passive browsing or environmental exposure.
  2. Exposure trigger & impulse activation — immediate situational cues (hunger, sales).
  3. Purchase — rapid execution paired with rationalization.
  4. Post-purchase — brief gratification fading into doubt.
  5. Reflection / aftermath — strong guilt and tracking avoidance.

Strategic Insight

To prevent app avoidance, interventions must introduce gentle micro-friction at the trigger / activation stage — not rigid end-of-month reports.

Phase 3 — Intervention Design & Assumption Testing

Testing the riskiest assumption first.

Ideation & experiment selection

Working with the team, I developed pro/con analyses and storyboards for potential interventions:

01

Cash-only physical budgets

Strong physical friction, but failed to address digital / online impulse behavior.

02

Daily intentions + AI-guided reflection (selected)

Highly adaptable, emotionally resonant, and scalable.

Rapid assumption testing & product pivot

To test the riskiest hypothesis — “impulse spenders actively want to stop impulse spending” — I organized and executed Assumption Test 1: a rapid quantitative/qualitative study (N = 14).

57.1% of participants expressed a clear desire to completely stop impulse spending — far from the near-universal motivation we’d assumed.

Strategic Team Pivot

I presented these findings to the team, and they fundamentally changed our strategy — pivoting Sage away from an aggressive “habit-breaking app” toward a non-judgmental Financial Wellness & Mindful Life Guide.

Phase 4 — Interaction Design & Brand Strategy

From research requirements to a calming system.

System architecture & wireframing

Translating research requirements into structural designs, I wireframed the core interactive dashboard flows:

  • Structured entry points for daily intention-setting, real-time spending checks, and impulse reflection cards.
  • Collaborated with the team to define user story maps and functional requirements for the MVP.

Behavioral brand strategy

I defined the behavioral rationale connecting Sage’s visual identity directly to user psychology:

01

Palette & atmosphere

Muted blue/green gradient meshes that evoke calm, reducing the financial anxiety typical of traditional banking apps.

02

Typography & voice

An expressive serif header (Cormorant Garamond) paired with a clear body font (DM Sans) to convey reflection without cognitive fatigue.

Phase 5 — Usability Evaluation & Final Product Space

Evaluating, iterating, and landing the vision.

Prototype usability testing

I designed and led the usability testing framework for the interactive prototype — task-based testing evaluating success rates, navigation clarity, and AI conversational tone.

Key Finding & Iteration

Users initially misinterpreted the AI chat as a standard customer-service tool. I redesigned the navigation hierarchy to elevate the AI chat as the primary landing space, adding clear status indicators for context-aware prompts.

Final vision & strategic impact

Sage addresses a critical gap in personal finance by shifting the paradigm from rigid tracking to context-aware emotional reflection. By intervening at the moment of impulse with non-judgmental AI coaching, Sage bridges the gap between financial intentions and daily spending actions.
