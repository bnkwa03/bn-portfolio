Limbo

01Problem & Audit

After graduation, friendships lose the proximity that quietly kept them alive.

When young adults scatter across cities, staying in touch degrades into high-effort catch-up calls or silent scrolling. Foundational interviews with recent grads mapped three barriers: high activation energy, no shared daily context, and a fear of being overbearing.

The open space

No incumbent combined a daily prompted ritual with a genuinely private, low-exposure circle. Broadcast apps push performance; private spaces lack a reason to show up. Limbo sits in the gap.

02Research Pipeline

A four-stage pipeline, with the method matched to each stage of product maturity.

Needfinding Interviews

90-minute qualitative deep dives with recent grads

11participants, 2 led solo
What I did

Ran 90-minute interviews with recent Stanford and SDSU grads, leading two deep-dive sessions individually.

Mapped the emotional friction of staying in touch once forced proximity disappears.

What I learned

Three barriers kept surfacing: high activation energy, no shared daily context, and a fear of being overbearing.

Private Spaces in Public Media Survey

How young adults construct digital intimacy

23respondents
What I did

Distributed across Reddit and community mailing lists to separate public broadcasts from private friend circles.

What I learned

Public feeds feel performative. Intimacy lives in low-exposure spaces like Close Friends and Finstas, where people share unpolished, everyday moments.

Interactive Preference Sorter

Head-to-head prompt ranking, paired with interviews

18+ follow-up interviews
Research engineering · built with Claude

I built the ranking tool instead of waiting on engineering.

Rather than block on a full build, I stood up a custom interactive sorter in Claude to run head-to-head preference rankings across four prompt categories — Hypotheticals, Opinions, Personal, and Debates — each test paired with a 1-on-1 interview. It gathered quantitative preference data in days, not weeks.

03Insights & Mechanics

Intimacy for one-on-one, play for groups.

What I found

One-on-one interactions needed intimacy; group settings needed playfulness and low pressure.

What we built
  • Private comments open a 1-on-1 DM instead of a public thread.
  • Playful input modes: a drawing canvas, debate sliders, and group game modes.

Personal and opinion prompts beat generic icebreakers.

What I found

In the sorter, Personal and Opinion prompts ranked far above generic icebreakers, which felt clinical and awkward among existing friends.

What we built

A master prompt taxonomy of surprising, specific angles over basic interview questions — the source of the launch pool.

04Go-To-Market & Launch

A research-backed campus takeover, with a generative brand.

Generative AI brand. Prototyped a bold, high-contrast identity with AI image tools — mood collages for posters, apparel, and pop-ups.

Poster takeover. Printed 24 top-ranked prompts and posted them overnight across grad housing, restrooms, and library stacks.

Interactive QR entry. Each poster let students answer instantly and see live campus responses, no download needed.

05Impact & Reflection

Limbo shipped 0-to-1 on the iOS App Store, with research driving the mechanics, the prompt pool, and the launch.

What I took away

0-to-1 systems thinking. A social product needs the whole ecosystem: behavioral research, UI mechanics, brand atmosphere, and real-world onboarding.

Proactive research engineering. Building my own tools, like the Claude sorter, let me gather preference data fast without waiting on a full build.

Limitations

Unmeasured long-term retention. The 22-week capstone timeline constrained 30-day longitudinal tracking.

Early needfinding sample. Deep-dive interviews leaned on a small set (2 of 11 led solo), validated through the broader survey and beta feedback.
