01Problem

Budgeting tools either explain spending after it happens or ask for effort people do not sustain.

Our first concept also assumed that people wanted impulse spending stopped. Mapping existing tools by when they intervene and how much work they require showed an opening for a low-effort tool that acts closer to the purchase itself, provided that assumption held.

WHY THIS METHOD

Convenience purchases happen too often to recall accurately, so logging them in the moment captured the behavior without distorting it.

WHAT IT REVEALED

Whether a purchase felt problematic depended on identity and values more than on impulsivity, so an unplanned purchase was not necessarily an unwanted one.

Rapid assumption test

14respondents
8wanted to reduce
5strongly
WHY THIS METHOD

The whole concept rested on the premise that people wanted impulse spending eliminated, which made it the highest-risk assumption to test before building further.

WHAT IT REVEALED

Only five of fourteen strongly wanted to reduce impulse spending, which was enough to stop designing Sage around restriction.

AI field intervention

7participants
5days in the field
2check-ins daily
WHY THIS METHOD

A reflective coach, which I designed to ask why rather than to shame, had to be tested in daily life to see whether it changed how people felt about spending, how they spent, or both.

WHAT IT REVEALED

Awareness changed faster than behavior, and declaring purchases in the morning created more useful friction than reflecting on them at night.

Formative usability test

2participants
4tasks
3redesigns
WHY THIS METHOD

A medium-fidelity test could show whether users understood the plan, spend, and reflect loop before the team committed to building it.

WHAT IT REVEALED

The coach did not feel central, the daily loop was not legible, and users could not tell whether a purchase had saved.

Coach felt like customer support→ Coach becomes the home screen
Daily loop was not legible→ Onboarding teaches the loop
No sign that a purchase saved→ Progress and save feedback
03Impact

Sage shifted from fixing behavior to supporting agency, and four principles carried that shift into the product.
