01Problem

Early childhood researchers could not figure out how to use an internal research tool.

iFind, an AI-assisted video editor, was created to aid intervention methods in the FIND Program. It was meant to speed up the processing of parent-child interaction videos into coaching videos that highlight program-specific, developmentally supportive techniques, but adoption of the software was less than 10%.

03Key Insights

Users lacked confidence in AI automation without control.

Observation

Users repeatedly expressed being uncomfortable moving forward with the task because they wanted to evaluate the source of the provided AI clip suggestions.

Fix

Grant users autonomy in reviewing the serve and return instances identified by AI, with playback of each instance and manual override for clip selection if desired.

Conceptual confusion about FIND terminology hindered understanding of the task.

Observation

The first 6 users all expressed confusion toward the stated goal. They were unsure how to identify the specific developmentally supportive technique because of nomenclature inconsistencies in the introductory presentation.

Fixes
  • I identified the erroneous use of both “Response” and “Return” in the script to describe “Serve and Return,” and standardized all instances to “Return” to align with lab terminology.
  • I created a graphic illustrating “Naming,” the technique users were tasked to identify, also showing what “Serve and Return” meant in the same context.

None of the 20+ participants afterward had conceptual understanding issues.

10+ usability violations identified, some of which include:

Violation
What happened
Fix
Hidden notifications
Notifications came in from and stayed at the bottom of the screen, and users missed them.
Create notifications that clearly grab users’ attention and match their mental model of notifications in similar systems.
No visibility of system status during download
The download button would desaturate, but nothing else showed whether anything was happening, leading to multiple clicks and multiple downloads.
Show a clear downloading status that prevents multiple downloads, and consider limiting downloads from repeated clicks less than a second apart.
Unclear hierarchy in the first steps
“Upload local video” and “Upload study video” seemed to do the same thing, and users were unsure which to click.
Condense the CTAs into one clear “Upload video” button.
Stale search bar
The search bar along the top bar had no obvious use, and curious users who tried it found that nothing happened.
Eliminate the search bar.
04Biggest impact from insight

The combination of identified usability violations and uncovered mental model mismatches led to iFind being adopted and regularly used by 100% of the FIND research team after 5 weeks of testing.

<10% → 100%adoption across the FIND research team
3×iteration speed, from 12 users per iteration to 4
05Future work

This work was completed in late 2023 and early 2024, early in my UXR career. Today, I would prioritize setting KPIs to measure impact quantitatively, along with more concrete measures of intuition in usability studies, such as first-click analysis, task completion rate, and time to complete the task. That would have made it easier to see how each change affected the user experience across iterations, rather than only the overall increase in adoption, even though adoption was the initial goal.
