Designing context-aware AI that meets impulse spending in the moment it happens.

Executive Summary

Existing personal finance platforms focus on numerical budgeting and post-hoc expense tracking — but impulse spending is overwhelmingly driven by real-time emotional states, cognitive fatigue, and environmental context.

Sage is a conversational AI financial coach designed to curb impulse spending by fostering mindful awareness, real-time reflection, and proactive intention-setting.

As Lead UX Researcher, I drove the qualitative and quantitative research roadmap — authoring research protocols, executing baseline and assumption testing, establishing behavioral personas, and leveraging those insights to pivot the team’s product strategy toward non-judgmental AI coaching.

Phase 1 — Problem Definition & Competitive Positioning

Grounding the strategy, then finding the white space.

Research frameworks

I analyzed core behavioral literature and synthesized two frameworks to ground the team’s product strategy:

01

Behavioral drivers

Evaluated research on how environmental triggers (sales notifications) and psychological states (FOMO, stress, fatigue) override rational spending plans.

02

Intervention mechanisms

Mapped research on proactive micro-friction and reflection to address the root drivers of impulse spending.

Competitive analysis

01

Market audit

Compiled a broad audit of digital budgeting platforms (including HoneyDue and Penny) and manual tracking habits, such as physical post-it-note logging.

02

Framework design

Defined a 2×2 competitive matrix mapping Timing of Intervention (reactive vs. preventative) against User Effort Required (passive vs. active).

03

White-space identification

Identified an unaddressed gap in the preventative + low-friction guided space — the rationale for a point-of-impulse conversational tool.

Phase 2 — Baseline Research & Behavioral Insights

Capturing how impulse spending actually happens.

Baseline study execution

01

Screening protocol · N = 39

Created screening criteria to recruit active convenience spenders making 2–3+ daily discretionary purchases.

02

Diary study · N = 7

Designed 5-day logging prompts, interview scripts, and participant communications to capture spending moments in real time.

03

Data synthesis

Led team affinity-mapping sessions to analyze logged impulse incidents across food, delivery, online retail, and subscriptions.

Persona: the Guilt-Driven Impulse Buyer

Synthesized baseline data to establish the primary behavioral persona:

01

Profile

Young adults and students (ages 20–30) experiencing high emotional volatility around spending.

02

Behavioral loop

Spontaneous purchases feel justified in the moment but trigger post-purchase guilt, leading to tracking avoidance.

Journey mapping

Mapped the 5-stage behavioral loop:

  1. Buildup → passive browsing or environmental exposure.
  2. Trigger / activation → immediate situational cues.
  3. Purchase → rapid execution with rationalization.
  4. Gratification → brief satisfaction fading into doubt.
  5. Guilt & avoidance → shame and tracking avoidance.

Core Takeaway

Post-purchase tracking triggers shame and app avoidance. Interventions must introduce micro-friction at the trigger / activation stage — before the transaction occurs.

Phase 3 — Assumption Testing & Strategic Pivot

Testing the riskiest assumption, then pivoting on the evidence.

Rapid assumption testing · N = 14

I tested the riskiest hypothesis — “impulse spenders actively want to stop impulse spending” — through a rapid quantitative/qualitative study.

57.1% of participants expressed a clear desire to completely stop impulse spending — far from the near-universal motivation we’d assumed.

Strategic Pivot

I presented these findings to the team to pivot Sage away from aggressive “habit-breaking” mechanics toward a non-judgmental Financial Wellness & Life Guide.

Final product vision

Most personal finance tools treat spending purely as a numerical problem, but impulse buying is deeply tied to emotional and environmental context. Sage bridges the gap between financial intentions and daily spending actions by providing a space for quick, non-judgmental reflection at the moment of impulse. By replacing rigid budgeting limits with mindful awareness, Sage establishes a sustainable framework for long-term behavior change.

Strategic Impact

Transformed baseline behavioral insights into a validated product space that addresses impulse spending at the point of decision — avoiding the guilt-and-avoidance cycle typical of traditional financial apps.
